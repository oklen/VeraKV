\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon agents accumulate interaction histories that outgrow the context window
and must be offloaded to an external memory. Most memory systems hand the reader what they constructed---facts, graphs,
summaries---even when they also keep the raw history, and constructions paraphrase away the exact values and steps that dense agent trajectories
are queried for; a growing line of work instead serves the raw history from a
cheap index. What is missing is measurement: \emph{which} property of raw payloads
carries the benefit, and \emph{when} the trajectory's own KV cache can stand in for
re-encoded text. This paper is a controlled study of both questions, shipped with a
reproducible system.

On payload form, matched-evidence ablations on dense agent trajectories---the same
cited evidence presented as verbatim spans, original-line extracts, LLM summaries, or
atomic facts---show that \emph{generative paraphrase} of matched evidence costs
$8$--$14\pp$, and that in the deployed pipeline deterministic extractive shortening costs about half of what generative re-encoding does,
extending controlled memory-representation comparisons to agent traces and separating
paraphrase damage from compression damage. On KV reuse, we distinguish computational
fidelity from task utility. Within the window, gathering the selected spans'
cached KV at their original positions is computationally faithful to a
full-prefill-then-mask oracle and \emph{accuracy-equivalent} to re-prefilling the
same spans as text---we claim a cost property, not an accuracy advantage: first-token
latency decouples from evidence size and beats a resident prefix-cache baseline.
Beyond the window, where no joint prefill exists, independently encoded event stores
collapse on the official benchmark ($-13\pp$ paired), two known repair families
recover $72\%$, and two scoped regularities organize the rest: evidence converts to
accuracy only through joint encoding, and a store's write-time conditioning must
match what is served, in content and in position---in a six-way selector comparison for the
fresh-recompute budget, no content signal, including a KEEP-style attention-propagation selector,
beats recency. Where the agent's own rollout cache survives, harvesting it
replaces the $5\times$ store-encoding cost at parity (8B/32B, Qwen/Llama,
AMA-Bench/LOCOMO), and a mixed deployment policy matches the routed-text carrier
baseline overall---the gain concentrated in the in-window harvest slice, the
over-window gap left open and stated.

The findings ship as \kvm{}, a verbatim-first memory stack over self-hosted
inference: ranked \#1 on the AMA-Bench leaderboard as of 2026-07-15 and competitive
on LOCOMO and LongMemEval-S, with all code, configurations, prompts, predictions,
and judge outputs released.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/oklen/VeraKV}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A long-horizon agent's state is its history: the tool calls it issued, the
observations it received, the reasoning it wrote. As that history grows past the
model's context window, the agent needs a memory. The prevailing answer is to
\emph{construct} one---to run an LLM over the trajectory that extracts atomic
facts (Mem0~\citep{mem0}), writes and links notes (A-Mem~\citep{amem}), distills
hierarchical summaries (MemoryBank~\citep{memorybank}), or builds a temporal
knowledge graph (Zep~\citep{zep})---and then retrieve over what was constructed. Many of these systems keep the raw history as well (Mem0, for one, stores the original messages); what matters is what reaches the reader. Retrieval returns the constructed units---Mem0 returns only its extracted facts---so which details the reader can ever see is fixed at write time, before any question exists.
This is natural for \emph{dialogue} memory, where the salient
content is a handful of stable user facts. It is a poor fit for \emph{agent
trajectories}, where the answer is often an exact value or a specific step that a
fact-extractor paraphrases away. AMA-Bench
\citep{amabench} makes this concrete: purpose-built dialogue-memory systems
(Mem0 $0.21$, A-Mem $0.32$, MemGPT $0.33$, MemoryBank $0.34$) all score
\emph{below} a no-memory long-context baseline ($0.52$) on dense agent
trajectories, and the benchmark's own needle control locates two losses: serving MemoryBank's constructed memory of the answer-bearing turns instead of the turns themselves drops accuracy from $0.46$ to $0.27$ ($-41\%$ relative, BabyAI), and similarity retrieval then misses those turns (HippoRAG2 loses a further $43\%$ end-to-end). What reaches the reader---not whether the raw history was kept---is where these systems lose.

A second line of work already draws the natural conclusion and serves the raw
history from a cheap index: gist navigation with raw-passage lookup
(ReadAgent~\citep{readagent}), full-fidelity trace databases indexed by summaries
(Memex~\citep{memexrl}, HORMA~\citep{horma}), budgeted verbatim evidence capsules
(EMBER~\citep{ember}), optical indexes with deterministic verbatim return
(OCR-Memory~\citep{ocrmemory}), and---concurrently with this work---a controlled
comparison showing verbatim chunks beat extracted artifacts on dialogue benchmarks
by $16$--$22\pp$~\citep{verbatimchunks}. ``Serve the raw text'' is no longer a
contrarian stance; it is a design pattern. What the pattern still lacks is
\emph{attribution} and a \emph{cost map}. Which property of the raw payload carries
the benefit---and is it paraphrase or mere shortening that does the damage? The
reader consuming the memory is a separate axis that end-to-end comparisons leave
entangled with the store~\citep{memdelta}; how much of a system gap is reader
prompting? And since the trajectory was prefilled once anyway on self-hosted
inference (vLLM~\citep{vllm}, SGLang~\citep{sglang}), when can the resulting KV
cache---the model's own reading of the evidence---be \emph{served} in place of
re-encoded text, and when does that reuse break? Throughout we keep three notions
separate that are easy to conflate: \emph{source fidelity} (is the payload's text
unaltered?), \emph{computational fidelity} (does served KV reproduce the
full-prefill computation?), and \emph{task utility} (does the answer come out
right?)---the first two never imply the third, and we measure each on its own
terms. Source fidelity is about \emph{tokens}, not information: a served row's keys and
values are verbatim those of the original tokens, but because the row was contextualized
during prefill it can carry causal influence from tokens the query never selected
(\S\ref{sec:oow}). We therefore say the memory serves selected \emph{rows}, not selected
\emph{information}, and are careful not to conflate the two.

We answer these questions with controlled experiments organized around \kvm{}, the
verbatim-first memory stack we build and release (\S\ref{sec:method}); the stack is
the instrument and the artifact, not a claim of a new memory primitive. Concretely:
i) \emph{payload-form attribution on dense agent trajectories}: matched-evidence
ablations in which only the payload form varies show that re-encoding the
\emph{same} selected evidence generatively costs $8$--$14\pp$ while, in the deployed pipeline, a deterministic
swap to its original lines costs about half as much as a generative re-encoding ($-2.6$ vs.\ $-5.1$/$-5.9\pp$)---extending controlled
memory-representation comparisons~\citep{verbatimchunks} from dialogue to agent
traces and separating \emph{paraphrase} damage from \emph{compression} damage, with
the effect largest where answers are exact (\S\ref{sec:ama}, \S\ref{sec:analysis});
ii) \emph{reader--memory separation as measurement discipline}: holding the memory
fixed, a one-sentence answer-instruction swap moves accuracy more than any of
fourteen reader-side mechanisms, and the answering protocol can travel inside the
memory's return value---applying concurrent skill-KV transplantation
work~\citep{kvnotes} to the agent-memory answering protocol at text-instruction
parity, then extended to a query-routed skill \emph{library} in which additive per-class
hints add a further $+6.3\pp$ on routed questions while replacement routing regresses
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}, \S\ref{sec:reader}); iii) \emph{a validated
within-window KV serving implementation}: position-preserving gather of selected
spans reproduces a full-prefill-then-mask oracle (under stated cache/position
assumptions) and is \emph{accuracy-equivalent} to re-prefilling the same spans as
text---the benefit we claim is cost, not accuracy: per-query prefill decouples from
evidence size, beating text re-prefill and a resident prefix-cache baseline on TTFT
(\S\ref{sec:kvserve},~\S\ref{sec:eff}); iv) \emph{an out-of-window
characterization}---the study's core: independently encoded event stores collapse
on the official harness ($-13.2\pp$ paired, $n{=}2431$), repair families adapted
from RAG-oriented cache splicing recover $72\%$ but their parity claims do not
transport, two scoped regularities organize the failure (evidence converts to
accuracy only through joint encoding; write-time conditioning must match serving in
content and position), no content-signal selector in a six-way comparison---including a KEEP-style
attention-propagation selector~\citep{keep}---beats recency for the
fresh-recompute budget, and where the agent's rollout cache survives, harvesting it
replaces the $5\times$ store-encoding cost at parity across scales (8B/32B),
benchmark families (AMA-Bench/LOCOMO), and backbones (Qwen/Llama); a mixed
deployment policy then matches the routed-text carrier baseline overall, its gain
concentrated in the in-window harvest slice, the over-window gap left open
(\S\ref{sec:oow}); and v) \emph{a reproducible system}: code, configurations,
prompts, raw predictions, and judge outputs for every reported run; the memory
stack alone (fixed default reader) already matches the purpose-built AMA-Agent, and
the full disclosed system is ranked \#1 on the public AMA-Bench leaderboard as of
2026-07-15, while remaining competitive on LOCOMO and LongMemEval-S
(\S\ref{sec:ama}, \S\ref{sec:locomo}, \S\ref{sec:lme}).

\section{Method: \kvm{}}
\label{sec:method}

\kvm{} is a KV-cache memory manager for self-hosted long-horizon agents. It
realizes one principle---deliver the evidence faithfully, never construct a lossy
store---through two operations: \emph{select} the raw spans a query needs, and
\emph{serve} them verbatim. Selection is one layer; \emph{serving} splits into
three regimes that this paper keeps explicitly distinct (Table~\ref{tab:modes}):
\emph{text rehydration} (general; produces every headline accuracy number),
\emph{whole-trajectory KV gather} (exact, only while the trajectory fits the
positional window), and \emph{per-event anchored KV stores} (the over-window
regime, approximate by necessity; characterized in \S\ref{sec:oow}). When a result
below depends on which regime served it, we say so at the point of use, and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} maps every reported number to its pipeline. We make the
pieces precise below (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:kvm},
Table~\ref{tab:config}).

\paragraph{Setup.} A trajectory is a sequence of segments (turns)
$T=(s_1,\dots,s_n)$, where $s_i$ carries a token string $x_i$; write
$x=x_1\Vert\cdots\Vert x_n$ for their concatenation, $N=\sum_i|x_i|$, and
$\mathrm{span}(i)=[\ell_i,r_i)$ for the token interval of $s_i$ in $x$
($\ell_i=\sum_{j<i}|x_j|$). The reader $f_\theta$ is a frozen autoregressive
transformer with rotary position embeddings (RoPE). Building the memory prefills
$x$ \emph{once} into a key--value cache
\[
  \mathcal{C}=\{(k_t,v_t)\}_{t=1}^{N},\qquad
  (k_t,v_t)=f_\theta\text{'s key/value at position }t\text{ attending to all }t'\le t,
\]
and attaches to each cold segment an extractive gist $g_i=\textsc{Head}(s_i)$ (a
prefix of its action/observation, not an LLM summary). The memory is
$M=(\{s_i\},\{g_i\},\mathcal{C},\{\mathrm{span}(i)\})$: the gist is a routing
index and a reader-facing navigation scaffold, but the \emph{authoritative}
evidence---what any query is answered from---is always the raw $x_i$.

\paragraph{Select: which spans a query needs.} Given a query $q$ and a token
budget $B$, retrieval forms three tiers. The \emph{hot} set is the last $h$
turns, $H=\{n{-}h{+}1,\dots,n\}$. The \emph{pin} set is the turns $q$ names by a
structural address---``step $N$'', ``turns $N$--$M$''---dereferenced
deterministically, $P(q)=\{i:\mathrm{addr}(q)\ni i\}$. The remaining cold turns
$\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{cold}}$ are scored by a pluggable router $R(q,s_i)$ and
taken greedily under budget, pins ranked first:
\[
  \mathcal{S}(q)=\operatorname*{top\text{-}}k_{\,i\,\in\,P(q)\cup\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{cold}}}\;R(q,s_i)
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}(q)}|x_i|\le B,
  \qquad \mathcal{K}(q)=H\cup\mathcal{S}(q),
\]
with $\mathcal{K}(q)$ kept in temporal order. $R$ ranges over lexical overlap,
dense cosine (with a lexical pre-filter), and their reciprocal-rank fusion; the
pin is a structural cue, not a scorer. Agent trajectories are event logs with
stable addresses (step indices, tool-call ids, file paths), and $82\%$ of
AMA-Bench questions carry at least one (step indices alone in $73\%$), so the
memory dereferences a named address deterministically rather than forcing all
access through similarity. How
much the router matters is regime-dependent: on step-indexed traces a cheap
router saturates, while on diffuse dialogue it carries more of the work
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{Serve: verbatim spans, natively as KV.} The selected spans are served
verbatim, and their native form is the cache. Rather than re-encode them, we
\emph{gather} their cached key--values at the original positions,
\[
  \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)}=\Big\{(k_t,v_t)\in\mathcal{C}\ :\ t\in\textstyle\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{K}(q)}\mathrm{span}(i)\Big\},
\]
and decode the answer by re-prefilling only $q$ on top of
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)}$. This gather is \emph{exact}: because RoPE makes
attention depend only on the relative offset $t-t'$, keeping each retained token
at its original position reproduces a full-prefill-then-mask oracle,
\[
  f_\theta\big(\cdot\mid \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)},\,q\big)
  \;=\;
  f_\theta^{\mathrm{mask}}\big(\cdot\mid x,\ \mathcal{K}(q),\ q\big),
\]
where $f_\theta^{\mathrm{mask}}$ prefills all of $x$ but masks the decode's
attention to the retained positions $\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{K}(q)}\mathrm{span}(i)$.
Crucially each gathered $(k_t,v_t)$ was computed while attending to earlier
\emph{unselected} turns, so it carries trajectory-conditioned context that a
compact re-prefill of $x_i$ in isolation cannot reconstruct---\S\ref{sec:analysis}
measures the residual to the oracle at $\sim\!0.7$ logits, versus $\sim\!13.5$ for
the isolated re-prefill. The per-query prefill is therefore $O(|q|)$, independent
of the evidence size $\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}(q)}|x_i|$, which the text path
re-prefills in full (\S\ref{sec:eff}). When a single observation overflows the
budget, a query-focused filter keeps only its query-relevant \emph{lines},
verbatim; retained evidence is always verbatim, even if not every line of an
oversized turn is retained.

\paragraph{The answering protocol travels in the return value.}
\texttt{retrieve()} returns a context the reader consumes, so it can carry more
than evidence. \kvm{} optionally prepends a fixed, query-independent
\emph{answering protocol} $\pi$ to that context---framed as a memory-service
note, with the host reader's instruction untouched. As a fixed prefix, $\pi$ is
prefilled once at position $0$ and reused, and it recovers most of a reader-side
instruction's benefit from inside the memory (\S\ref{sec:analysis}). It is off by
default.

\paragraph{Assembly.} Two modes share the store. The \emph{text pipeline}, which
produces all headline numbers, returns
\[
  C(q)=\big[\,\pi\ \Vert\ O\ \Vert\ (x_i)_{i\in\mathcal{S}(q)}\ \Vert\ q\,\big],
  \qquad a\sim f_\theta\big(C(q)\big),
\]
where the overview $O$ is the hot turns verbatim plus every cold turn's gist and
is prefilled once per episode. The \emph{KV-serving} mode drops $O$ and serves
$[(x_i)_{i\in\mathcal{K}(q)}\Vert q]$ through $\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)}$. Both
keep each turn's explicit \texttt{<step $N$>} tag, so temporal order is read from
tags, not physical position.

\paragraph{Scope of the cache-native path.} KV gather is exact only when the source
turns were prefilled within the model's positional window; the text path carries no
such assumption and is the general path, in particular for the over-window
trajectories this paper targets---AMA-Bench \textsc{Software} runs $60$--$100$k tokens
against a $22$k budget. Table~\ref{tab:modes} states the three regimes: text
rehydration produces every headline number, full-trajectory KV gather is exact only
within the window (the \S\ref{sec:eff} prototype), and chunked KV gather is a systems
extension.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\kvm{}: build once, then select-and-serve per query. The gather step
returns the full-prefill KV of the retained positions, so the KV decode
reproduces $f_\theta^{\mathrm{mask}}$ (\S\ref{sec:method}) while re-prefilling only
$q$; the text pipeline assembles the same selection with an overview.}
\label{alg:kvm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{BuildMemory}{$T=(s_1,\dots,s_n)$}
  \State $x \gets x_1\Vert\cdots\Vert x_n$;\ \ record $\mathrm{span}(i)=[\ell_i,r_i)$ for each $s_i$
  \State $g_i \gets \textsc{Head}(s_i)$ for each $s_i$ \Comment{extractive gist; index only, never payload}
  \If{$N \le$ positional window}
    \State $\mathcal{C} \gets \textsc{Prefill}_{f_\theta}(x)$ \Comment{one joint prefill: exact KV serving available}
  \Else
    \State $\mathcal{C} \gets \{\textsc{EncodeAnchored}_{f_\theta}(s_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ \Comment{per-event anchored stores, approximate (\S\ref{sec:oow}); text path needs no $\mathcal{C}$}
  \EndIf
  \State \Return $M=(\{s_i\},\{g_i\},\mathcal{C},\{\mathrm{span}(i)\})$
\EndProcedure
\Statex
\Procedure{Retrieve}{$M,\ q,\ B$}
  \State $H \gets \{n{-}h{+}1,\dots,n\}$ \Comment{hot: last $h$ turns}
  \State $P(q) \gets \{i : \mathrm{addr}(q)\ni i\}$ \Comment{pin: structural addresses in $q$}
  \State $\mathcal{S}(q) \gets \operatorname*{top\text{-}}k_{\,i\in P(q)\cup\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{cold}}} R(q,s_i)$\ \ s.t.\ $\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}(q)}|x_i|\le B$
  \State $\mathcal{K}(q) \gets H\cup\mathcal{S}(q)$ \Comment{keep in temporal order}
  \If{KV-serving}
    \State $\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)} \gets \{(k_t,v_t)\in\mathcal{C} : t\in\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{K}(q)}\mathrm{span}(i)\}$ \Comment{gather at original positions}
    \State \Return $f_\theta(\cdot \mid \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}(q)},\, q)$ \Comment{re-prefill only $q$}
  \Else
    \State $O \gets [\,\text{hot verbatim} \Vert (g_i)_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{cold}}}\,]$ \Comment{overview, per-episode}
    \State \Return $f_\theta\big(\cdot \mid [\,\pi \Vert O \Vert (x_i)_{i\in\mathcal{S}(q)} \Vert q\,]\big)$
  \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate on three long-term-memory families that stress different inputs:
\textbf{AMA-Bench}~\citep{amabench} (agent trajectories), \textbf{LOCOMO}~\citep{locomo}
(multi-session dialogue), and \textbf{LongMemEval-S}~\citep{longmemeval} (long-context
chat). We follow each benchmark's official protocol---same reader and judge, its own
metric. For AMA-Bench this is the official \texttt{run.py} harness on the full
open-ended set, backbone and judge both Qwen3-32B~\citep{qwen3} (``Track B''), 22k
context, so our numbers drop into the published leaderboard; unless noted, every
AMA-Bench result uses this default and every ablation contrast is
same-batch.\footnote{The open-ended set is 208 episodes / 2{,}496 QA over six domains.
LOCOMO and LongMemEval-S reader/judge protocols are stated where we report them.}

\paragraph{Statistical inference.} Questions within an episode share a trajectory and
are not independent, so every interval we report is an \emph{episode-clustered}
bootstrap CI (episodes resampled as clusters; $208$ on the full AMA set), and paired
McNemar discordant counts are reported as descriptive effect summaries alongside those
CIs rather than as stand-alone tests. Contrasts fall in three classes, labeled at the
point of use: \emph{confirmatory} (decision rule fixed before the run, e.g.\ the
deployment-map evaluation of \S\ref{sec:oow}), \emph{exploratory} (developed
iteratively on the public set, e.g.\ the leaderboard configuration), and
\emph{post-hoc mechanism analyses} (the \S\ref{sec:oow} dials); no multiple-testing
correction is applied to the exploratory class, and we rely on replication---across
scales, benchmark families, and backbones---rather than corrected $p$-values for the
mechanism claims. Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} maps every headline number to its
pipeline, prompt, sample size, and batch.

\subsection{AMA-Bench: leading results on agent-trajectory memory}
\label{sec:ama}

Table~\ref{tab:ama} places \kvm{} on AMA-Bench. With only a \emph{free lexical}
router---no learned index, no construction---\kvm{} already matches the purpose-built
AMA-Agent ($0.5621$ vs $0.5722$) and beats every other published memory system by

this risk in the same way: their contrasts were pre-registered with fixed decision

Where the gains come from is the subject of
\S\ref{sec:analysis}.\footnote{The $0.5954$ memory-only run carries a $95\%$

$60$--$100$k-token trajectory held verbatim: the one place AMA-Agent's
whole-trajectory state summary, written at construction time, genuinely helps.\footnote{The
\textsc{Software} mechanism is dissected in Appendix~\ref{app:swmech}.}

\subsection{LongMemEval-S: routed selection beats truncation under budget pressure}
\label{sec:lme}

LongMemEval-S puts each question over its own $\sim$115k-token, $\sim$50-session
haystack; we evaluate the \emph{full} $500$ questions ($470$ answerable $+\,30$
abstention), with $95\%$ bootstrap CIs, on the official
\texttt{longmemeval\_s} release.\footnote{xiaowu0162/longmemeval; the oracle
evidence-session labels are used \emph{only} for error attribution between selection
and reader failures, never by the method; the exact file and access date ship with
our artifacts.} In the Qwen3-32B regime two facts hold.
(i) When the history \emph{exceeds} the window---the operative case for long-horizon
agents---the full arm must truncate, and routed selection at a fraction of the budget
beats it substantially: $0.496\,[0.453,0.543]$ vs $0.302\,[0.262,0.343]$ at a 28k budget
($+0.194\,[0.149,0.238]$). The mechanism is clean: truncation drops old evidence
sessions ($95\%$ of its answerable misses are \emph{selection} failures), whereas the
router keeps the gold session in budget ($9\%$ selection-fail). (ii) When the history
instead \emph{fits} an extended 128k window (via YaRN), full context wins overall
($0.564\,[0.519,0.609]$ vs $0.496$; $+0.068\,[0.019,0.119]$)---but at $4.5\times$ the
context, and its edge is confined to \emph{recall/breadth}: full leads on single-session
recall (ss-user $0.828$ vs $0.609$, ss-asst $0.946$ vs $0.768$) and multi-session
($0.405$ vs $0.322$), while routed selection \emph{wins} temporal-reasoning ($0.331$ vs
$0.291$) and knowledge-update ($0.736$ vs $0.708$; Fig.~\ref{fig:lme}). So
``selection $\geq$ full'' is \emph{regime-dependent}. Here the router \emph{is} load-bearing (unlike on AMA): the
dense-embedding hybrid beats a lexical router ($0.496$ vs $0.453$; $+0.043\,[0.006,0.079]$),
entirely by cutting selection failures ($9\%$ vs $23\%$).

\subsection{Where the gain comes from: payload fidelity, not the router}
\label{sec:analysis}

It would be tempting to credit a clever router. A same-batch
memory$\times$reader factorial (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}) says otherwise. The
verbatim-selection substrate accounts for most of the memory-side gain---\emph{just selecting raw spans}, with a
token-overlap router and the harness's default reader, already matches the
purpose-built system ($0.5601$ vs $0.5722$), and the full memory stack under that
same default reader beats it ($0.5954$, $+2.3\pp$). Routing refinements are
second-order: the fused second router signal adds $+1.4\pp$ as originally
measured (audited and superseded in Appendix~\ref{app:routeraudit}) and the structural step-pin
$+1.7\pp$, consistently at either reader ($+3.1$--$3.5\pp$ total). The
single largest effect is not in the memory at all: swapping the reader's
one-sentence answer instruction for a structured one adds $+5.1$--$5.5\pp$ at
either router, and the two axes are additive to within batch noise (same-day
replicate $\pm0.5\pp$). Memory systems ship with very different readers
(AMA-Agent an agentic tool loop, MemGPT its own control loop), so end-to-end gaps
that do not control the reader conflate the two effects; we report them
separately. Router \emph{sophistication} remains regime-dependent: its payoff scales with how
lexically un-addressable the evidence is---$\approx\!0$ on step-indexed agent
trajectories (audited, Appendix~\ref{app:routeraudit}), rising to $+4.3\pp$ on long
chat (LongMemEval-S) and $+12.6\pp$ on diffuse dialogue (LOCOMO), where it is the
only thing that lifts selection above full context. Below we localize this memory-side win to the \emph{fidelity} of the
payload, show that serving that payload as cached KV rather than re-encoded text
is faithful in a different, \emph{computational} sense---one that carries a cost
advantage but, we find, no accuracy advantage---and treat the reader as a separable axis
whose answering protocol can be served from the memory itself.

\paragraph{Substrate, then structure, then semantics.}
On AMA-Bench specifically, three memory-side effects separate cleanly
(tabulated side by side in Appendix~\ref{app:amaextra}). The \emph{substrate}---serving selected raw spans verbatim in a filled budget,
even with a free token-overlap router---is the bulk of the gain, lifting us from the
constructed-memory field into AMA-Agent territory; selecting raw spans alone is not, since AMA-Bench's own raw-turn baselines score $0.34$--$0.42$ (\emph{Versus standard vector-RAG}, below). Semantic router
\emph{sophistication} is second-order at best (audited below).
The remaining lever is not a smarter scorer but a one-line \emph{structural} cue:
$73\%$ of AMA-Bench questions cite a step index (``at Turn~38'', ``across steps
28--30''), and pinning exactly those steps adds $+1.7\pp$---and does so
\emph{entirely} on the step-citing questions ($+2.5\pp$ on them vs exactly
$+0.0\pp$ on the rest), a clean mechanism signature: on step-indexed agent traces
the order is substrate~$\gg$~semantic router~$\approx$~structure, and the structural
pin is a transparent rule, not a learned exploit.

\paragraph{The deployed router, audited.}
The router in the deployed stack fuses lexical overlap with a second
``model-pick'' signal. A controlled full-set audit finds that signal
\emph{score-neutral}: paired within one batch, a lexical base $+$ step-pin ($0.6466$) differs from
the model-pick hybrid base by $-0.8\pp$ $[-2.3,+0.8]$; with its pick call fixed, the signal stays score-neutral.\footnote{Full audit in
Appendix~\ref{app:routeraudit}.} We therefore name and release the reproducible
\textbf{lexical $+$ step-pin} default---the substrate and the pin carry the routing
on AMA-Bench, not a semantic scorer.

\paragraph{Robustness of the step-pin result.} The step-pin is a transparent bonus
on the step-citing subset, not the source of the result: deleting the step number
from the query costs content-based selection only $-0.2\pp$, corrupting the pin
address degrades gracefully rather than catastrophically, and a different-family
Llama-3.1-8B judge preserves every ordering.\footnote{Appendix~\ref{app:amaextra}.}

\paragraph{Controlled payload ablation (oracle evidence).}
The cross-system $+10$--$35\pp$ above conflates payload form with chunking, prompt, and budget. We
isolate the payload with a within-system test: on step-citing questions we take the \emph{exact cited
step} as oracle evidence (holding retrieval fixed) and present that same evidence three ways---the raw
\emph{verbatim} step, an LLM \emph{summary}, or LLM-extracted \emph{facts}---to the same
reader, prompt, and judge. Only the payload form varies, so the gap is causal (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload}):
verbatim beats an LLM summary by $+10.1\pp$ and extracted facts by $+8.5\pp$ over $910$ QA, widening to
$+14\pp$ on exact-recall (type A) where lossy forms drop the exact value. This is the controlled version
of that result: matched evidence, matched reader, lossy re-encoding \emph{alone} costs
$8$--$14\pp$. To rule out the reader grading its own generations leniently, a \emph{different-family}
judge (Llama-3.1-8B) re-scoring the same answers preserves the ordering---verbatim $34.3\%$ $>$ facts
$30.2\%$ $>$ summary $29.8\%$, still $+4$--$5\pp$---so the verbatim advantage is not a judge artifact.
(These absolute numbers are not comparable to Table~\ref{tab:ama}: this stripped
setting gives the reader only the single cited step---no overview, recent turns, or neighbors.) A \emph{same-pipeline} version closes that gap in scale: keeping the full
deployed context and replacing only the router-selected verbatim appendix with LLM summaries of the same
steps (same QA, overview, router, reader, and judge; the swap fires on $96\%$ of questions) costs
$-5.1\pp$ ($0.578$ vs $0.629$, same-day). Table~\ref{tab:family} completes the family under
same-batch anchors: swapping the appendix to Mem0-style \emph{extracted atomic facts} costs
$-5.9\pp$, and both generative re-encodings score at or \emph{below} serving no appendix at all
(gist-only $-4.3\pp$)---a lossy re-encoding of the selected evidence adds nothing over the one-line
gists already in the overview. The control that separates \emph{compression} from \emph{paraphrase}
is a deterministic swap to the query-relevant \emph{original lines} of the same steps (no LLM, no
rewording): it costs only $-2.6\pp$, about half the generative cost in the same pipeline. So lossy \emph{generative}
re-encoding is far more harmful than extractive compression---which still costs
$-2.6\pp$---and in this pipeline the choice is verbatim (whole spans or extracted
original lines), not construction.

\paragraph{The non-selected floor is second-order; payload fidelity is the lever.}
Routed selection and the gist overview are two \emph{separate} assembly choices, so we
cross them in a same-batch $2\times2$ (Table~\ref{tab:assembly}, Appendix~\ref{app:assembly}).
At the deployment
budget the four cells are statistically indistinguishable---every paired contrast is
within $0.9\pp$ with McNemar $p>0.28$. The gist overview is a navigation scaffold, not a payload: adding it to plain
recency changes accuracy by $-0.4\pp$ (n.s.), with or without selection. The routed
verbatim appendix adds little \emph{at this lexical router}---it has not yet found the
right spans---but its value scales with selection quality, reaching $-4.3\pp$ to remove
at the deployed router$+$pin (Table~\ref{tab:family}). The variable that moves
accuracy is therefore neither the overview nor the bare presence of an appendix, but the
\emph{fidelity} of what selection returns: the same routed spans served verbatim rather
than as an LLM summary are worth $+10.1\pp$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload}). Representation of
the floor is second-order; fidelity of the selected payload is first-order. This
selection-versus-overview split is domain-dependent---selection helps retrieval-heavy
Web, \textsc{Software}, and \textsc{Text2SQL} and slightly hurts holistic Game and
OpenWorld---which we break out in Appendix~\ref{app:assembly}.

\paragraph{Versus standard vector-RAG.}
The embedding router \emph{is} a standard vector-RAG retriever---it embeds each
step's full text and takes cosine top-$k$, the same first stage as AMA-Agent, whose released code
likewise re-injects the retrieved raw steps (next to an LLM-written state summary). Verbatim re-injection is
therefore not, by itself, what separates \kvm{}: AMA-Bench's own raw-turn baselines also return raw steps (BM25, top-$10$: $0.3436$; Qwen3-Embedding, top-$5$: $0.4227$~\citep{amabench}). On a shared subset our routers order
$\text{lexical }0.557 < \text{vector-RAG }0.583 < \text{RRF hybrid
}0.600$---the router moves accuracy by about $4\pp$---while all three sit far above
those raw-turn baselines and every constructed-memory system (Table~\ref{tab:ama}). That gap is cross-pipeline and mixes budget,
assembly, and prompt, so we do not attribute it to verbatim re-injection alone;
\kvm{} adds tiering, structural pinning, and KV reuse on top of a standard retriever.

\subsection{Serving the evidence as KV: the model's own reading}
\label{sec:kvserve}
The payload-fidelity result has a limit: text re-encoding, however verbatim, is
still a \emph{fresh} encoding of the evidence. The faithful ideal is the model's
\emph{own} reading of it---the keys and values it already computed over the
trajectory---and because \kvm{}'s store is a KV cache, we can serve exactly that.
Selection decides \emph{which} evidence reaches the reader; the KV cache decides
\emph{how faithfully} it is carried, and these are the two halves of \kvm{}.
Because the store is a KV cache, selecting is a cache operation: we prefill the
trajectory once and, per query, gather the selected spans' keys and values
\emph{at their original positions} and decode only the question. Each span's KV
was computed \emph{in situ}, while the model attended to everything before it, so
it holds that span's trajectory-conditioned reading. Position-preserving gather is
therefore exact under the conditions it relies on---identical cached keys and
values, retained \emph{original} position ids, and a standard causal RoPE
transformer (mechanisms that read absolute position, sliding-window or sink masks,
state-space mixing, cache quantization, or paged relocation fall outside the
guarantee)---so the sub-selected prefill reproduces a
\emph{full-prefill-then-mask} oracle: first-token argmax matches on every query
($100\%$ across synthetic and $144$ real AMA trajectories, $24/24$ episodes), and
greedily decoding $256$ tokens it emits the identical sequence as the oracle at
both $3$k- and $27$k-token trajectories (logit-KL $\le\!0.011$; \S\ref{sec:eff}).

\paragraph{The exactness survives to graded answers.} The oracle match is at the
logit level; it also holds end to end. On the $112$ real AMA episodes that fit the
prefill window ($\le\!26$k tokens---five of the six domains, since
\textsc{Software}'s $60$--$100$k-token trajectories cannot be prefilled whole,
cf.\ Table~\ref{tab:modes}), we route the \emph{same} spans per query and answer all
$896$ questions \emph{both} ways---gathering the selected KV at its original
positions versus re-prefilling those spans as text---then grade both with the same
Qwen3-8B judge. Accuracy is at parity: $17.3\%$ from the cache versus $17.4\%$ from
text ($\Delta\,0.1\pp$, one question in $896$; each question type within $\pm2\pp$),
with $83\%$ first-token argmax agreement and the mask-oracle faithfulness gate
holding throughout ($126/128$). The absolute level reflects a stripped
$8$B harness ($k{=}5$ selected spans, no overview), not the headline setting;
what the test isolates is that the two serving paths are \emph{indistinguishable
downstream}. Serving the model's own cached reading is therefore not merely
logit-equivalent to re-encoding the evidence, it is \emph{accuracy}-equivalent
wherever the trajectory fits the window.

\paragraph{The cache carries context that re-encoded text cannot.} This is where a
KV memory departs from a verbatim-\emph{text} one. Re-encoding the same selected
span as text \emph{in isolation}---the compact pipeline---starts fresh: its tokens
never attended the dropped surroundings, so its keys and values are not the ones
the full model computed. Measured against the mask oracle, gathered KV sits
$\sim\!0.7$ logits away (bf16 non-determinism), while a compact text re-prefill of
the same span sits $\sim\!13.5$ logits away---about $20\times$ farther
(Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}). The gathered KV therefore carries
trajectory-conditioned context that no re-encoding of the span in isolation can
reconstruct. A direct causal test confirms the cache carries this and text cannot:
perturbing only the \emph{unselected} upstream turns and re-gathering the
\emph{same} selected spans changes the gathered-KV answer on $66\%$ of queries
($111/168$ real AMA QA, Qwen3-8B), while a text re-prefill of those spans---blind
to the perturbed turns---is invariant \emph{by construction} ($0\%$), the gather
staying faithful to the mask oracle throughout ($24/24$). This is the deeper reading of ``deliver evidence faithfully'': the
cache is the model's own reading of the evidence, served unaltered---and it is
what the name \kvm{} refers to. Everything above holds within the window, where every
served row descends from one joint prefill; \S\ref{sec:oow} charts what survives beyond
that boundary, where no joint prefill exists and exactness is impossible in principle.

\subsection{The reader is a separable axis}
\label{sec:reader}
Reader comprehension is a separate axis that memory research usually leaves
entangled with the memory---systems ship with very different readers, so a gain
credited to a memory can be a reader change instead. Holding the reader fixed, the
two axes prove asymmetric: a memory error is unrecoverable downstream, while every
reader-side deficit we found is repairable at answer time by one sentence. Stress-testing
fourteen reader-side mechanisms over a fixed memory---plans, compiled views,
checklists, re-retrieval, type-specialized instructions---all come back null or
negative once routing reaches $\sim\!100\%$ recall; the single lever that reaches
emission is the one-sentence \emph{answer instruction} ($+5\pp$), and even that is
answer-completeness, not extra reasoning.\footnote{Full 14-mechanism falsification map
in Appendix~\ref{app:readermap}.}

That one lever is itself \emph{serveable}: prepending the structured answering
protocol to the front of the retrieved context---a fixed-position segment prefilled
once under KV serving, leaving the host instruction untouched---transfers most of the
gain ($+2.3$ to $+3.8\pp$ over same-batch anchors). In the out-of-window store regime
(\S\ref{sec:oow}) the transfer becomes exact: the instruction encoded \emph{once} as a
global skill block (no episode content) and spliced per query with zero forward
passes---its keys RoPE-rotated to sit contiguously before the question---ties the text
instruction on both the anchored-store and the harvested-cache bases ($-0.4\pp$,
$p{=}0.86$; $+0.1\pp$, $p{=}1.0$; full $+4.5$/$+8.1\pp$ lift transmitted; the parity
replicates on LOCOMO at 8B and 32B, \S\ref{sec:oow}), while the same
block frozen at a far position transmits only a fraction of it. The transplantation
mechanism itself---precompiled KV made position-portable by RoPE
repositioning---is established concurrently by \citet{kvnotes} across twelve models;
what we add is the memory-side characterization: the protocol travels as a
retrievable object of the same store that serves the evidence, at answer-accuracy
parity on official-scale memory QA. The deployed system holds a \emph{single} fixed protocol
block per benchmark. Whether a query-routed \emph{library} of blocks beats that single
block is a question the same splice answers directly, and we now run it: eleven skill
blocks, regex-routed per question with the universal structured answer protocol as fallback,
served as rotated KV. It resolves the routing question our falsification map had left
open. The \emph{replacement} form still fails---swapping the universal instruction for
type-specialized fragments regresses $-3.0\pp$ with ten collateral losses per targeted
fix (App.~\ref{app:readermap}). But the \emph{additive} form wins: a per-class hint
appended to the intact protocol lifts accuracy $+2.1\pp$ overall ($p{=}0.009$,
$n{=}800$) and $+6.3\pp$ on the $34\%$ of questions where a rule fires (up to $+10.8\pp$
on the largest routed class, $n{=}130$; per-class figures are descriptive, from a single
fixed rule set rather than a swept design), whereas replacement over the same rules stays a
wash ($+3.7\pp$ routed, $p{=}0.27$) and double-edged ($+12.5\pp$ on one class, $-15\pp$ on
another). Served as KV the blocks show no detectable gap from their text form across all
three variants ($|d|{\le}1.8\pp$, all $p{\ge}0.25$)---an absence of a detected difference,
not a formal equivalence---so the library precompiles into the same store, though the
KV-carried additive gain is only directional at this scale ($+0.8\pp$, $p{=}0.46$), leaving
the text carrier the demonstrated lever.
The store's answering skill is therefore not a fixed constant but a routed, extensible
object of the memory: additive routing a real headroom lever, replacement a trap. Placement, not context,
is what a skill needs: it never attended the evidence and works at full strength---the
mirror image of evidence blocks, which demand native positions and context-conditioned
encoding.

\subsection{Efficiency: KV sub-selection decouples cost from evidence}
\label{sec:eff}

Serving spans from the cache rather than re-encoding them is correct by
construction---position-preserving gather reproduces the full-prefill computation,
an equivalence that holds across attention kernels, a $4\times$ YaRN extension, and a
non-Qwen backbone.\footnote{Correctness gauntlet and end-to-end accuracy-neutrality
against the text pipeline in Appendix~\ref{app:kvcorrect}.} We measure its
\emph{cost} as a single-request microbenchmark. Because the sub-selected decode
re-prefills only the question, its time-to-first-token is \emph{flat} while the text
path grows with the selected evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:eff}): on a dense Qwen3-8B the
per-query speedup rises from $1.6\times$ to $10.9\times$ as evidence grows to
$\sim\!4$k tokens, reaching $32\times$ at $32$k, and the one-time trajectory prefill
saves $\sim\!2$M prefill tokens over a full AMA run. The decoupling is a property of
the gather, not the weights---a Llama-3.1-8B backbone shows the same flat TTFT.

Two fairness notes on this comparison. First, the speedups are measured against
re-prefilling the selected evidence as text; the strongest within-window alternative is
a \emph{resident full-trajectory prefix cache} (standard prefix caching), which also
re-prefills only the question---against it, sub-selection's saving is not the prefill
but the attention context (every question and decode token attends $E$ selected tokens
rather than all $N$), plus the option to evict unselected rows; measured on the same
protocol ($N{\approx}14$k resident rows, $K{=}2$--$12$), the sub-selected question
reaches first token in $40$--$48$\,ms versus $79$\,ms over the resident full cache
($1.4$--$1.9\times$) and $140$--$369$\,ms for text re-prefill, decodes $26\%$ faster
per token ($32.7$ vs.\ $41.2$\,ms/token), and the gather itself costs $3$--$6$\,ms. Second,
the saving is in the \emph{prefill}, and it is bought with memory: the resident KV
grows at $0.144$\,MB/token ($9.4$\,GB for a $64$k-token episode). A like-for-like
comparison against production paged-attention stacks needs a serving-system
integration, which we leave to future work.

\section{Out of the window: when can agent memory reuse {KV} caches?}
\label{sec:oow}

Within the window the cache path is exact because every served row descends from one joint
prefill (\S\ref{sec:kvserve}). Beyond it, exactness is impossible in principle: once the
history outgrows the window, events must be encoded without their future and often without
most of their past, and any such store is a different computation from the full reading.
This section charts what survives the crossing. Throughout, arms are paired per question
(exact McNemar tests, episode-clustered bootstrap CIs), all arms see the same routed
evidence unless the contrast itself is evidence visibility, and the pipeline is
deterministic---the deployed configuration reproduced byte-identically nineteen times across
independently written harnesses.

\paragraph{Stores, and the repair.} Each event (agent step span) is encoded offline into a
per-event KV store and a query selects $K$ of them (\S\ref{sec:method}); the stored rows are
assembled at their \emph{original} positions behind the shared header and the question is
decoded over them. The text path (\emph{tx}) re-prefills the same routed evidence as text.
The naive store (\emph{iso}) encodes each event in isolation. The repaired store
(\emph{anchored+tail}) instantiates two known repair families as agent-memory write/serve
operations---we adapt and characterize here; the primitives and their combination are prior
art. At write time, \emph{anchored encoding}~\citep{star}: each event is encoded as
[header; episode opening ($w{=}4096$ tokens, truncated at the event start, at its native
positions); event], keeping only the event rows. At query time, \emph{selective
recomputation}~\citep{cacheblend,epic}: the opening's rows not covered by selected events
are served alongside, and the last two events are recomputed freshly over the assembled
cache before the question.

\paragraph{Failure, repair, and the budget.} Table~\ref{tab:oow} is the headline. Isolated
stores lose $13.2\pp$ against text re-prefill of the same evidence---to our knowledge the
first large-sample \emph{paired} quantification on the official harness, though the
phenomenon itself is established~\citep{sparsex}, and our failing object is
construction-time independent encoding, not transplanted prefixes. The repaired store
recovers $72\%$ at $K{=}12$; at the matched lean budget $K{=}5$ the paired difference is
statistically indistinguishable from zero ($-0.8\pp$, CI $[-3.0,+1.4]$)---we do not call
this non-inferiority, since the margin such a claim needs was not pre-registered; what
the CI does exclude is anything resembling the naive failure. Beyond the window, where the text path must
re-prefill everything it serves, the cache path reaches parity at $12$--$18\%$ of the text
path's per-query fresh tokens. Costs are stated honestly: writing stores costs
${\sim}5\times$ one episode prefill (offline, event-parallel), repaid over-window and under
reuse, not within one episode's own questions; in-window TTFT is $1.4$--$2.3\times$ faster
but decode is ${\sim}29\%$ slower under the scattered layout, so end-to-end latency inverts
for answers longer than ${\sim}20$ tokens---the claim is \emph{reduces TTFT}, never
\emph{answers faster}.

\paragraph{Matched evidence, and the ceiling.} Two audits discipline the wins. First, the
cache path's within-window edge over routed text ($+5.6\pp$ at 8B) is \emph{not} a carrier
effect: served anchor rows are extra evidence, and giving the text path the same rows
(tx\textsubscript{plus}) recovers most of the gap ($+4.3\pp$, $p{=}0.001$ at 8B; $+5.5\pp$,
$p{=}0.004$ at 32B); at matched evidence the two carriers are at parity at both scales. The
episode opening is simply cheap evidence that $K$-routing systematically misses---the cache
gets it structurally, text must buy it with tokens. Second, against \emph{complete} reading:
at 8B the repaired store \emph{ties} a full re-prefill of the entire episode ($.221$ vs.\
$.220$, $p{=}1.0$) at ${\sim}22\%$ of its query cost---while rows \emph{gathered} from that
same full prefill score far below it ($.175$): exact rows minus answer-time global attention
lose what approximate-but-repaired stores reconstruct. Gathering itself is not the culprit:
give the gathered rows the same opening-region rows---all free from the same cache---and the
harvest path reaches statistical parity with the repaired store ($-1.1\pp$, $p{=}0.37$),
\emph{with no fresh tail at all} (the tail, worth $+3.9\pp$ on isolated stores, buys exactly
nothing on harvested rows, which already carry full-context integration). Where a rollout
cache exists, then, a \emph{zero-write} memory---gather routed spans plus the opening from
the agent's own run cache---matches the $5\times$-write recipe: exactly so at 32B
($.3313{=}.3313$, $+57/{-}57$ discordants, both the identical $5.3\pp$ below the strong
reader's full-reading ceiling), and robustly to prompt mismatch (harvesting from a cache
built under an agent-style system prompt costs $+0.1\pp$, n.s.). The pattern also crosses
benchmark families and scales: on LOCOMO ($n{=}1150$ paired, sessions as events) the
zero-write harvest exactly ties the text path at 8B ($+0.1\pp$, $p{=}1.0$) and sits
at-or-above the repaired store ($+2.0\pp$, clustered CI $[-0.1,+4.2]$), spending $40$
question-only fresh tokens per query against the text path's ${\sim}9$k; at 32B, where
the repaired store's published edge over text replicates to the decimal ($+3.9\pp$, CI
$[+1.9,+6.0]$, $p{=}8{\times}10^{-4}$), the harvest inherits it ($+3.4\pp$ over text,
$p{=}0.004$; $-0.5\pp$ vs.\ the store, $p{=}0.64$), and the answer instruction carried
as a rotated skill block matches its text-carried control at both scales ($+0.1\pp$
$p{=}1.0$; $+1.0\pp$ $p{=}0.24$). It also crosses backbones: on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
the full cell replicates---unrepaired global holes fall below the store ($-2.2\pp$),
the opening-repaired harvest sits at-or-above it ($+1.8\pp$, CI $[-0.2,+3.9]$), and the
fresh tail again buys nothing on harvested rows ($+0.2\pp$/$-0.5\pp$, n.s.).
What the write buys is the
over-window regime, where a live run truncates and no full-context cache ever exists. The
tie is reader-scoped: a 32B
reader keeps converting complete context into accuracy ($+9.1\pp$ over routed text,
$p{=}10^{-5}$), leaving every budget-limited serving---cache and text alike---below it and
at parity with each other. The residual is not degeneracy: in the official losses the cache
path's answers are $98\%$ fluent with wrong precise details (zero garbles; degenerate
outputs are equally rare in both paths), and distributionally the cached serving sits
\emph{closer} to the full-reading distribution than routed text does (median first-token
KL $0.005$ vs.\ $0.031$ nats)---losses are near-tied argmax flips, which is also why no
cheap per-question fallback trigger exists (six signal families, including running both
paths and voting, all fail to beat chance).

\paragraph{Regularity I: evidence converts to accuracy only through joint encoding.}
(Both regularities are empirical and scoped: one model family, this benchmark family,
these budgets.) Raising the
routing budget $K{=}5{\to}12$ moves the two carriers oppositely: text gains $+2.8\pp$
($p{=}0.0015$) while the cache is \emph{exactly} flat ($.3593{=}.3593$)---not insensitive
but self-cancelling ($+199/{-}199$ flipped answers). A within-episode factorial sharpens
this into the paper's cleanest controlled statement: handing the \emph{same} three extra
events to both paths, text gains $+2.6\pp$ while the cache \emph{loses} $-1.8\pp$
(difference-in-differences $+4.4\pp$, clustered CI $[+1.5,+7.3]$), and for the cache
\emph{any} extension hurts (random: $-1.65\pp$, $p{=}0.029$). Freshly re-read text is
jointly re-encoded with the question and the rest of the evidence; pre-encoded blocks that
never attended each other add retrievable-but-isolated facts plus attention dilution, and
the two cancel. Deployment follows: serve the cache \emph{lean} ($K{=}5$ serves ${\sim}40\%$
of the $K{=}12$ volume for free), and to close the residual add fresh computation or reader
scale, not served rows.

\paragraph{Regularity II: write-time conditioning must match what is served---in content
and in position.} Why does the anchor work? Not by injecting content: with serving matched and
equal-length conditioners, a true-episode prefix beats no conditioner by $+3.3\pp$
($p{=}0.009$), but another episode's text or even a \emph{shuffled} prefix performs
similarly, while degenerate fillers (a repeated token, a generated digest) score \emph{below}
no conditioner---the anchor primarily repairs the encoding boundary state (sink placement,
positional posture), and a wrong conditioner is worse than none. A \emph{note-transplant}
reading of this repair---prefill parks derived conclusions on delimiter/aggregator tokens,
and serving those rows would deliver them~\citep{kvnotes}---fails a direct test: serving
only the anchor region's delimiter rows does no better than matched random rows
($-0.7$/$+0.4\pp$ at $K{=}64$/$256$, both n.s.), both trail serving \emph{nothing} at
$K{=}64$, and the region's $+4.5\pp$ value ($p{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$) appears only as the
near-complete contiguous block---a scaffold-and-coverage effect, not sparse conclusion
notes; sparse rows spliced into an assembled cache make holes, they do not carry
conclusions. Two controlled dials then
bound the remaining semantic and geometric components. \emph{Content}: freezing one global
conditioner for all episodes---which would make writes prefix-cacheable, collapsing write
cost $5\times{\to}1\times$---costs $-3.4\pp$ ($p{=}0.007$): the same-episode content
contributes a real $2$--$3.4\pp$. \emph{Position}: sliding the anchor adjacent to the event
(the contiguous layout every splice-and-repair pipeline assumes) costs $-2.8\pp$
($p{=}0.0008$), while pushing the event $8$k tokens \emph{farther} away costs almost
nothing ($-1.7\pp$, n.s.), and distance mutes neither the benefit nor the poison of the
conditioner. The store works best when its write-time worldview matches the serve-time
layout---the opening at its true position, the hole between them real, the served anchor
rows the ones the store was conditioned on. Query-time token repair cannot substitute for
this write-time conditioning: an EPIC-style static boundary recompute~\citep{epic} at its
published budget is a null in this regime ($k{=}16$: $+0.2\pp$, n.s.), and even equipped
with our served anchor rows and fresh tail it trails the anchored store by $5.2\pp$
($p{<}10^{-4}$)---in agent traces the isolation damage is distributed over the whole
event's rows, not concentrated at its boundary. Nor does a \emph{smarter} conditioner
help: replacing the opening with each event's top dependency-graph upstream events
(budget-matched, native positions, their rows served) loses $-2.9\pp$ ($p{=}0.022$) and is
no better than random prior events---while serving the conditioner's own rows helps for
\emph{every} conditioner family ($+2.8$ to $+3.4\pp$), generalizing the consistency law.
The opening wins because, alone among the six write-side alternatives we tested, it is simultaneously natural text
(boundary repair), same-episode content, truthful geometry, the evidence block routing
systematically misses, and a constant fully-serveable block; the six write-side
alternatives we tested all lose to it.

\paragraph{The fresh budget: six selectors tested, recency stands.} The one query-time degree of freedom is
where the two-event fresh recomputation goes. At fixed evidence the tail helps immediately
and saturates ($+2.1\pp$ at two events, flat at six), and its value is not query relevance:
under matched evidence and budget, six selectors---recency, a signature-co-occurrence
dependency graph, query$\to$cache attention mass, a KEEP-style query-conditioned
attention propagation over events (the group-recompute selector of \citet{keep},
instantiated at our event granularity), query relevance, random---leave recency tied or
ahead. The propagation selector lands exactly on the random floor ($+0.2\pp$ vs.\ random,
$p{=}0.89$) and significantly below recency ($-2.1\pp$, clustered CI $[-3.5,-0.7]$,
$p{=}0.016$), despite spontaneously agreeing with recency on $1.2$ of its $2$ picks;
query-selected excerpts are marginally \emph{worse} (third replication of that null). The
selectors do pick different events; the differences do not pay. Recency is not a lazy
default but the causally downstream integration point, and the operating point is thereby
six-way validated rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Deployment map.} The recipe splits by \emph{where the rows come from}, not by
what is served. In-window, where the agent's own rollout cache survives, harvest it: no
store encoding, no fresh tail, robust to the run-vs-review prompt mismatch, at exact
parity with the re-encoding recipe at both scales (\S above). Over-window, a live run
truncates and no full-context cache ever exists---there, and only there, the $5\times$
write buys anchored stores that are window-independent. In both regimes the reader's
answering protocol travels as a rotated skill block from a global store
(\S\ref{sec:reader}), and the two memory content types obey mirrored serving rules:
evidence rows are position-preserving and context-hungry; skill rows are
position-independent, query-adjacent, and context-free. Route by domain, not per
question: on the official judge the
cache path wins \textsc{Embodied} outright at both budgets ($.19$--$.21$ vs.\
$.12$--$.13$) and text keeps \textsc{OpenWorld}; with domain routing the
indistinguishable-at-$K{=}5$ headline becomes a win where traces are dense
state--action loops. Reader capacity
moderates everything: on LOCOMO the repaired cache beats text by $+3.9\pp$ at 32B
(conversation-clustered CI $[+1.9,+6.0]$) while 8B regresses on single-hop near-details;
$77\%$ of those regressions are answered correctly at 32B under the same protocol and
memory content. The end-to-end deployment-map system---harvest$+$skill in-window,
anchored stores$+$skill over-window---was then run once on the full official harness
against the pre-registered paired contrasts: over $n{=}2431$ paired questions it scores
$.397$ against the anchored-store recipe's $.360$ ($+3.6\pp$, clustered CI $[+1.7,+5.6]$,
$+304/{-}216$ discordants, $p{=}1.3{\times}10^{-4}$) and \emph{exactly ties} the
routed-text carrier baseline of Table~\ref{tab:oow} ($.397$ vs.\ $.397$, $+285/{-}287$
discordants, $p{=}0.97$)---the serving-cell gap closes; the full deployed pipeline
(different protocol, Table~\ref{tab:ama}) is not the comparator here. The gain lives on the in-window slice ($43\%$ of
questions, $+6.4\pp$, $p{=}9{\times}10^{-5}$), where the zero-write harvest serves the
rows and the skill block delivers the answering protocol; there the KV-carried skill
also matches its text-carried control ($+1.6\pp$, n.s.), and the over-window slice is
unchanged ($+1.5\pp$, n.s.).

\paragraph{Four synthetic probes of what a served row carries.} The regularities above are
attribution claims about a served row---what computation it still embodies once detached
from its origin. Four controlled probes make the mechanism direct (Qwen3-8B, synthetic
register-tracking traces after RULER variable tracking~\citep{ruler}, exact-match scored,
arms paired), spanning the four ways a row is produced: one continuous prefill
(\emph{full}), one joint prefill then \emph{gather} the wanted rows (\emph{harvest}),
independent per-event encoding $[\text{header};E_i]$ (\emph{iso}), and a rolling anchored
write $[\text{header};\text{opening};E_i]$ (\emph{anchor}). \textbf{(1)~Phantom conclusions.} Two equal-length prefixes carry opposite states; the
\emph{same} event text (``the status from step~1 was copied into the report'') is
teacher-forced after each, and we serve \emph{only} that event's rows---identical token
IDs, positions, header, and row count across the two arms, so only the invisible donor
differs. Across $72$ such donor pairs, both arms separate in their donor's polarity
direction in $64\%$ of pairs (each arm alone follows its own donor $81\%$ of the time)
against an $8\%$ no-donor floor, while the donor's \emph{exact} token is reproduced only
$10\%$ of the time (significance taken over the $72$ pairs, not the $144$ arms)---a served
row is a lossy carrier of the \emph{conclusions} its prefix induced, not merely a cache of
its own visible tokens (attention-mediated memoized inference~\citep{kvnotes}, measured).
\textbf{(2)~Erasure boundary.} That carry is not \emph{unconditional} leakage. Deleting a
numeric value's source rows from a joint prefill and then querying the current state, the
model answers ``not specified'' exactly as an oracle that never saw the value ($0/96$; an
overwriting event cleanly supersedes, $96/96$): no \emph{detectable} behavioral leakage
under explicit overwrite and current-state queries. We claim only that---$0/96$ bounds the
leakage rate below ${\sim}4\%$ and says nothing about logit-level residue, arbitrary
queries, or exact state-equivalence to a fresh prefill (\S\ref{sec:limits}). Read with~(1),
the picture is selective: a prefix's influence surfaces when the model formed a
task-relevant conclusion in the suffix \emph{and} the query reads it, not whenever the
prefix was present. \textbf{(3)~Sink versus dependency.} On a three-event register chain a shared header lifts
multi-hop resolution from $0.20$ to $0.48$ ($p{=}2{\times}10^{-11}$) but leaves it short of
full context's $0.72$ ($p{=}6{\times}10^{-8}$); single-fact retrieval shows no improvement
($0.18$ vs.\ $0.14$, both far under full's $1.00$---a retrieval floor under independent
encoding, not a null interaction). A shared prefix therefore substantially but only partially
restores cross-event reasoning over independently encoded rows; it does not \emph{store} the
cross-event edge, the residual to full being the cost of the missing joint encoding, which is
why sharing a prefix (Star-style~\citep{star}) addresses repeated-sink placement yet not the
missing inter-event conditioning. What in the header carries this---sink normalization and
positional posture versus the variable and instruction semantics it also supplies---we do not
separate here; an equal-length sink-only header would, and we leave that open.
\textbf{(4)~Where the harvest gain comes from.} A four-arm decomposition on the same
trajectory-spanning chains settles the attribution (max sequence $589$ tokens, so full's
failure is distraction, not truncation; exact- and number-recall scoring agree to the digit).
Serving only the gold chain reaches $0.98$--$1.00$ however it is carried---fresh compact text
($1.00$), independently encoded rows ($1.00$), or rows harvested from one joint prefill of the
full trajectory ($0.98$)---while full context sits at $0.04$. The whole gap is
\emph{routing/denoising}: selected-text $-$ full $={+}0.96$ ($+368/{-}0$, $p{<}10^{-100}$).
\emph{Pre-digestion} is null---harvest does not beat a clean re-encode of the same chain
($-1.8\pp$, if anything slightly worse for having attended the distractors)---and
\emph{joint conditioning} is null on the routed chain---harvest ties independent encoding
($-1.8\pp$), because once only the relevant events are co-served the reader re-derives the
chain at serve time regardless of carrier. So under \emph{complete} routing harvest buys
\emph{cost}, not computation: a near-lossless carrier of the selected rows (within $2\pp$ of
fresh text), the accuracy lever being distraction removal, which any selective serving
delivers. Its globally computed rows out-earn a fresh re-encode only in one narrow
corner---when routing is \emph{incomplete} and the missing content is a coarse, explicitly
copied label. Dropping the step that introduces the target and serving only a later row that
copied it: a binary state survives almost perfectly---but our first two measurements of that
cell were both wrong in instructive ways, and the corrected chain is worth stating. The
original design drew the served decoy as the gold label's negation; at $K{=}2$, ``recall the
memoized value'' and ``answer the opposite of the served decoy'' are indistinguishable, and
the arm read harvest $0.99$ against $0.00$ for independent encoding. Redrawing the decoy
\emph{independently} ($n{=}256$) exposes fresh text and independent KV as pure decoy
echoers---both score ${\approx}0.5$, exactly the rate at which an independent decoy happens
to coincide with the gold---and puts elimination-free recall at $r{\approx}0.80$. That $0.80$
was in turn a \emph{readout} limit, not a storage limit: naming the two candidate labels in
the question lifts recall to $0.934$--$0.984$ across three runs ($n{=}256$ each), the
cleanest being a cell with \emph{no} state word served anywhere---nothing to echo, nothing to
eliminate---where harvest scores $0.984$ against $0.54$ for both re-read text and independent
KV (coin flips, as they carry nothing). Entropy still closes the channel, and the menu
defends the boundary rather than dissolving it: with candidates named and decoys independent,
a four-way label sits at chance ($0.223$ vs.\ $0.25$; $92\%$ of answers echo the served
decoy), an eight-way label likewise ($0.156$ vs.\ $0.125$), and an exact three-digit value
never survives ($0/128$). A verdict that must be \emph{computed} rather than copied is not
pre-stored either ($0.48$, at chance under the same menu-style readout---though the model
derives it fine when the source \emph{is} served). The failure mode is uniform: when the row
cannot supply the answer, the reader echoes whatever label is served, which is also why
harvest is not uniformly better---on the subset where the independent decoy coincides with
the gold, the echoers score a lucky $1.00$ while harvest's residual recall failures cost it
($0.803$, $p{=}3{\times}10^{-8}$). What a row inherits also turns out to be sharply
\emph{local to its own predicate}, not a side channel for whatever preceded it: with a
verdict explicitly stated upstream and then dropped, a semantically neutral later row
(``checkpoint saved for the sensor review'') carries nothing ($0.47$, chance), and neither
does a row \emph{about} the verdict (``the outcome of the check was archived'', $0.52$)---but
a row whose own predicate \emph{binds} a state to that verdict (``the status flag was set
according to the check result'') carries it at $0.94$ ($n{=}256$ each, McNemar
$p{=}3{\times}10^{-33}$, identical upstream and question). A served row thus carries the
resolved value of the state its own predicate binds---roughly one bit of it---and nothing it
merely mentions, neighbors, or follows; mirroring probe~(1)'s polarity-yes / exact-token-no
split, now with a task payoff, while higher-entropy specifics and derived facts still require
their source rows. This is why the deployed harvest ties rather than beats re-encoded text
(\S\ref{sec:oow}): agent answers are usually exact values and derived facts, precisely the
content sparse serving cannot carry without its source. It is also a construction rule for
write-time notes: a note memoizes a conclusion only if its own predicate binds it (a paired
in-trajectory note reading ``determine the threshold verdict now and record it here''---no
answer, no operands---lifts post-drop recovery of a \emph{computed} verdict from $0.54$ to
$0.70$, $n{=}192$, $p{=}0.005$, while the same note leaves a three-digit value at exactly
$0$), whereas a note that merely refers to a conclusion stores nothing.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Agent-memory systems.}
The systems we compare to---AMA-Agent, Mem0~\citep{mem0},
MemoryBank~\citep{memorybank}, A-Mem~\citep{amem}, MemGPT~\citep{memgpt},
Zep~\citep{zep}, MemoRAG~\citep{memorag}, HippoRAG~\citep{hipporag}---all
\emph{construct} something at write time with an LLM (facts, notes, summaries, graphs, a compressed global memory), and most also keep the raw history; they differ in what the reader is served. Mem0 retrieves only its extracted facts; MemoRAG and HippoRAG use their constructed memory or graph to retrieve raw passages;
AMA-Agent's released code serves its top-$5$ raw turns by embedding similarity together with an LLM-written state summary, and falls back to keyword search over the raw trace. AMA-Bench's own needle control
prices serving the constructed form: replacing the raw answer-bearing turns with MemoryBank's constructed memory drops accuracy from $0.46$ to $0.27$ ($-41\%$ relative, BabyAI). Two precedents are closest to us.
\emph{Generative Agents}~\citep{genagents} retrieves by recency-importance-relevance
over a stream that mixes verbatim observations with summarized reflections, but
gives no verbatim-rehydration guarantee. \emph{MemGPT}~\citep{memgpt} pages
verbatim context via LLM tool calls but recursively summarizes overflow rather
than keeping it exactly rehydratable. \kvm{} differs by constructing no LLM-written memory at all: raw spans are the only evidence
payload, routed to by a cheap index, with extractive gists serving only as a one-line
index and overview scaffold---never as a substitute for evidence a query needs,
which is always rehydrated verbatim.
Crucially, all of these are query-aware, so query-awareness is not the novelty, and neither is serving raw spans as such (AMA-Agent does too);
the combination of a \emph{verbatim-only payload} $+$ \emph{cheap routing} $+$
\emph{gist-as-index} is.

\paragraph{Raw-history memory: an established design pattern.}
Serving the raw history from a cheap index is by now a settled pattern, and we
claim no priority on it. ReadAgent~\citep{readagent} navigates by gists and looks
up original passages on demand; Memex~\citep{memexrl} and HORMA~\citep{horma} keep
full-fidelity traces in databases indexed by summaries (with RL-trained retrieval);
EMBER~\citep{ember} retains budgeted verbatim evidence capsules;
OCR-Memory~\citep{ocrmemory} predicts segment indexes optically and returns the
original text deterministically; EM-LLM~\citep{emllm} segments streams into
episodic events at the KV level; filesystem-backed agents search stored transcripts
with tools~\citep{lettafiles,pamlocomo}, as does AMA-Agent's tool loop on agent
traces. Closest of all, and concurrent, \citet{verbatimchunks} run the controlled
comparison directly---fixed pipeline, verbatim chunks vs.\ LLM-extracted typed
artifacts---and report $15.9$/$22.0\pp$ gaps on LOCOMO/LongMemEval-S. Our Part I is
therefore an \emph{extension and a decomposition}, not a discovery claim: we bring
the controlled comparison to dense \emph{agent trajectories}, add the
original-line control that separates \emph{generative paraphrase} from
\emph{extractive shortening} (the artifact-vs-chunk contrast conflates them), add
an oracle-evidence variant and a different-family judge control, and pair it with
the memory--reader decoupling. Concurrently,
MemDelta~\citep{memdelta} makes the confound-control case for agent-memory
\emph{evaluation}---varying one pipeline component at a time on LongMemEval and
showing rankings reverse; our matched-evidence protocol is the payload-form
instance of the same discipline, and we adopt their recommendation to report
memory-only and end-to-end numbers separately (\S\ref{sec:ama}).

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented generation and agent frameworks.}
Classic RAG retrieves external passages and conditions the reader on
them---RAG~\citep{rag}, REALM~\citep{realm}, RETRO~\citep{retro},
Atlas~\citep{atlas}. \kvm{} is RAG turned \emph{inward}: the corpus is the agent's
own trajectory and the retrieved units are re-injected as \emph{verbatim KV}
rather than re-encoded text, so retrieval and reuse share one representation
(the ``standard vector-RAG'' point of this design is our EmbeddingRouter,
\S\ref{sec:analysis}). The memory modules practitioners actually deploy---the
buffer/summary/vector stores in LangChain~\citep{langchain} and
AutoGen~\citep{autogen}---occupy exactly the slot \kvm{} targets; it is a
drop-in, verbatim-first alternative to those summary/vector defaults.

\paragraph{KV reuse and compression.}
A systems line reuses KV caches for latency---prefix caching in
vLLM~\citep{vllm} and SGLang~\citep{sglang}---which \kvm{} sits on top of. A
compression line removes tokens via eviction (e.g.\ H2O~\citep{h2o}) or learned
summarization (RECOMP~\citep{recomp}); IntentKV~\citep{intentkv} prunes agent
KV across turns with a learned intent signal; these optimize the cache or the
prompt, not the \emph{selection-quality} memory question that determines whether a
multi-hop answer survives. Reuse of rollout KV also appears beyond language
agents---WorldKV~\citep{worldkv} re-inserts evicted cache chunks into the native
attention window of a video world model without re-encoding---and, concurrently
with this work, \citet{kvnotes} show precompiled \emph{skills} are
position-portable: RoPE-repositioned KV splices are near-indistinguishable from
full recompute across twelve models. Our skill-block result (\S\ref{sec:reader})
is an application of that transplantation idea to the agent-memory answering
protocol, validated at answer-accuracy parity on official-scale memory QA; the
mechanism is theirs, the memory-side characterization ours. A parallel architecture line compresses or retrieves
\emph{past activations} inside the model---Compressive
Transformer~\citep{compressivetransformer}, Memorizing
Transformer~\citep{memorizingtransformer}---whereas \kvm{} sits one level up, as a
training-free memory manager over the raw KV of a frozen model. Our point is
orthogonal and, where they overlap (at a
matched training-free, query-free budget), extractive retention is already at
least as good as generative summary---so the lever is selection, not harder
compression.

\paragraph{Position-independent KV caching.}
A recent serving line moves beyond prefix caching toward reusing recurring
\emph{spans}---documents, code files, retrieved chunks---even when they appear at
different prompt positions, which standard prefix caching cannot do (it reuses KV
only across an identical shared prefix, and only in the prefill).
EPIC~\citep{epic} formalizes position-independent caching and recovers accuracy
near segment boundaries; MiniPIC~\citep{minipic} supports it in a minimal vLLM
substrate by storing \emph{position-free} keys and applying RoPE at attention time
under per-request logical positions; MEPIC~\citep{mepic} makes reused chunk blocks
shareable across positions and requests to cut HBM; and HYPIC~\citep{hypic}
extends the idea to hybrid-attention models. Earlier, Prompt
Cache~\citep{promptcache} reused modular attention states and
CacheBlend~\citep{cacheblend} showed that non-prefix chunk KV must be partially
recomputed to stay faithful. \kvm{} is complementary and \emph{upstream}: these
systems assume the reusable spans are given and optimize their serving, whereas
\kvm{} decides \emph{which} spans of a long agent trajectory to select and shows a
verbatim payload beats a lossily constructed one. Our KV serving is also
deliberately restricted---it is \emph{position-preserving} (spans stay at their
original trajectory positions, exact for RoPE) rather than position-independent,
so it does not compact the resident KV; a memory-compact version that relocates
spans would need exactly the PIC mechanisms above (position-free keys, logical
positions, block/page sharing), which we do not claim to contribute.

\paragraph{Repairing spliced caches.}
When chunks are encoded independently and later spliced, a repair family restores
quality: anchor-block conditioning (Star Attention~\citep{star}), partial recomputation
of high-deviation or boundary tokens (CacheBlend~\citep{cacheblend}, EPIC~\citep{epic}),
and sparse-query selection with recency fallbacks over segment-level reuse
(SparseX~\citep{sparsex}, the nearest neighbor to \S\ref{sec:oow}; it also reports the
naive-reuse failure and a repair on memory benchmarks); KEEP~\citep{keep} moves
group-level recompute selection by query-conditioned attention propagation into an
embodied-planning memory system. In RAG-style regimes---
self-contained chunks, all chunks served---this family reports repair~$\approx$~full
re-prefill. \S\ref{sec:oow} instantiates the anchor and recompute families as
agent-memory operations under routed serving and finds the parity claims do not
transport: the boundary-recompute budget that suffices for RAG chunks is a null on agent
events, whose isolation damage is distributed across the whole event
(\S\ref{sec:oow}, Regularity II), and KEEP's propagation selector, run same-harness at
matched budget, lands on the random floor below recency. Our contribution relative to this family is the paired
official-benchmark characterization and the two regularities, not the primitives or their
combination.

\section{Limitations: when does verbatim selection win?}
\label{sec:limits}

\kvm{} defers answer-extraction to the reader at inference time, which draws a
clear regime boundary we chart rather than hide. Verbatim selection wins when the
answer is an \emph{exact, objective span buried in dense history} and the history
either overflows the window or drowns the reader in distractors---the setting of
AMA-Bench and, on dialogue, LOCOMO. It does not dominate everywhere: when the
whole history \emph{fits} the window and the reader can exploit it, full context
can win (LongMemEval-S at 128k, $+6.8\pp$ at $4.5\times$ the budget); when the
reader is \emph{weak} and the answers are tiny isolated facts, systems that
extract atomic facts at construction time can present an easier context. Both
crossovers are in our results (\S\ref{sec:lme}), and both are about reader
capacity and history length, not retrieval---on LongMemEval-S only $9\%$ of the routed
arm's answerable misses are selection failures (\S\ref{sec:lme}).

The two pillars have distinct boundaries. The \emph{KV} pillar's strongest
form---position-preserving sub-selection of arbitrary spans---needs a self-hosted
reader; hosted APIs expose only contiguous-prefix prompt-caching, which our
prefix-friendly layout still benefits from but which cannot gather arbitrary
spans. We therefore report the KV serving as a scoped prototype (\S\ref{sec:eff}; its
serving regimes and their trajectory-length limits are in Table~\ref{tab:modes}),
and report LongMemEval-S only in the strong-reader (Qwen3-32B) regime---a scoping
choice, testing the settings the claim is about.

The \emph{select} pillar's boundary is a \emph{coverage} ceiling, visible in one
domain. On AMA-Bench \textsc{Software}, selection achieves $100\%$ recall of the
\emph{named} steps yet AMA-Agent leads by $+17\pp$; the dissection in
Appendix~\ref{app:swmech} eliminates retrieval, tool access,
output format, and prompted iteration, and localizes the residual to
\emph{temporal-scan} questions whose answer step is unnamed and must be proven
extremal over the whole trajectory---so even supplied verbatim it caps a reader at
$\sim\!0.12$. Verifying an extremum needs the entire $60$--$100$k-token trajectory,
which AMA-Agent's construction-time state summary covers and a fixed retrieval
budget cannot. That is genuinely complementary, and our ablations indicate it
cannot be absorbed by bolting machinery onto a single-pass reader.

Finally, verbatim storage carries governance costs, as does any system that also keeps the raw history---which many constructed memories do.
The store retains raw history: PII persists verbatim and must be handled by
policy outside the memory (encryption at rest, retention windows, write-time
scanning). Deletion semantics differ by layer. In the \emph{text} substrate,
dropping a turn is exact---the next assembly simply omits it. In the \emph{KV}
layer it is not: cached keys/values are contextualized, so the KV of tokens
\emph{after} an erased turn was computed while attending to it, and exact erasure
requires invalidating or recomputing the downstream blocks (bounded, in our
per-episode caches, by the episode length), or learned steering states that localize
the edit without a full recompute~\citep{kveraser}. We measured whether that residue is
\emph{extractable}: planting a secret at one step, erasing it, and serving only
later steps, the gathered-KV arm emitted the secret in $0/120$ trials and its
first-token log-probability sat at the noise floor of an arm that never saw it
($\approx\!-31$ nats), while a positive control leaks ($120/120$)---so the
carryover is measurable at the logit level but did not surface erased content in
generation; a functional-erasure probe (\S\ref{sec:oow}) shows the same for state, not
just secrecy---with a deleted value's rows removed, generation matches an oracle that
never saw it ($0/96$). We still treat exact KV-layer erasure as requiring downstream
invalidation, make no claim under adversarial extraction, and have not evaluated
redaction pipelines.

The out-of-window study (\S\ref{sec:oow}) carries its own scope. Its conditioner and
geometry dials and fresh-selector comparisons are 8B-only ($n{=}824$) and presumed, not
shown, to transfer to 32B (the evidence-matching audit, the harvest cell, and the skill
block do replicate at 32B and on LOCOMO, and the harvest cell also on a Llama
backbone); the non-inferiority margin was
not pre-registered; the within-window 32B edge has a clustered CI touching zero; results
beyond those replications cover one model family (Qwen3) and uncompacted
position-preserving stores whose
per-token storage economics we do not measure; and end-to-end latency inverts for long
answers, so the cache path claims reduced TTFT only.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a long-horizon agent whose answers are exact, objective, and buried in a
dense trajectory---and whose history overflows the window or drowns the reader
in distractors---the right memory operation is to \emph{deliver evidence
faithfully}: construct indices, not answer payloads. \kvm{} keeps the raw
history as reusable KV, indexes and scaffolds it cheaply, selects the spans a
query needs, and serves them verbatim---within the window, from their cached KV,
a carrier computationally faithful to the model's own full-prefill reading and
accuracy-equivalent to re-encoded text at lower first-token cost. When the
history fits and the reader can exploit
it, full context can win; when the reader is weak and the facts are tiny,
construction can win; we state both boundaries. Within its regime, a small
framework built on this principle leads our runs of AMA-Bench (submitted and
leaderboard-verified as the top entry, \#1 as of 2026-07-15), is competitive with dedicated
dialogue-memory systems on LOCOMO, and traces the selection-vs-full-context
crossover on LongMemEval-S---with the win driven by payload fidelity rather than
router sophistication, the reader treated as a separable axis whose answering
protocol the memory can itself serve, and the cost amortized by KV reuse.
Beyond the window the same principle survives in a weakened, priced form: stores must be
written with a conditioning that matches how they will be served---the true opening, at
its true position---and served lean with a small fresh tail; what cannot be bought back
at any serving budget is the joint re-encoding a strong reader performs when it re-reads.
And where the agent's own rollout cache survives, even the write is unnecessary: the
memory harvested from that cache, with the answering protocol riding along as a rotated
skill block, ties the deployed text pipeline on the full official harness.
The recipe is simple enough to fork, and we release it, with configurations and
predictions included, as a reproducible baseline that centers payload fidelity
over memory construction.

\section{The three answer instructions, verbatim}
\label{app:instr}

The reader-side variable of \S\ref{sec:analysis} is a single swapped sentence.
For auditability we reproduce all three instructions exactly as run.

\paragraph{Default (harness).}
\begin{quote}\small
Provide a direct and concise answer.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Structured (ours; $+5.1$--$5.5\pp$ at either router).}
\begin{quote}\small
Answer using ONLY the context above. Break the request into sub-questions if
needed, answer each citing exact evidence (values, step indices, outcomes)
VERBATIM, then give the final answer.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Quote-first (probe; $-10.0\pp$ on \textsc{Software}, same batch).}
\begin{quote}\small
Answer using ONLY the context above. Locate the exact evidence first, then
answer by COPYING the exact strings from the context VERBATIM---commands, file
paths, code, values, error messages, step indices. Do not paraphrase or
re-describe them. The final answer should be the exact string(s) plus the
minimum wording needed to address the question.
\end{quote}

The comparison is itself a control: the structured and quote-first
instructions share the grounding preamble (``using ONLY the context above'')
and the same emphasis on verbatim evidence, and differ in what they ask the
reader's \emph{reasoning} to do---work through sub-questions versus copy
strings. The former gains $+5$--$6\pp$ overall, the latter loses $10\pp$ on its
probe domain: the structured instruction's value is the elicited sub-question
reasoning in the answering pass, not the grounding phrase or the verbatim
emphasis it shares with a losing variant.


\section{The reader-side falsification map}
\label{app:readermap}

This appendix carries the mechanism-level evidence behind the reader-saturation
finding of \S\ref{sec:analysis}; Table~\ref{tab:reader} tabulates all fourteen
mechanisms. On the flagship memory we tested: letting the model compress the
selected evidence before answering; a sufficiency gate fused into the answer
prompt that can trigger re-retrieval; the same gate as a separate call; a
query-conditioned entity-state timeline prepended as a scaffold; and
deterministic lookup tools (exact get-by-index, exhaustive search) over the
lossless store. None of these helps; several hurt, and the attribution is
exact: the fused gate's $-1.6\pp$ splits into $-2.6\pp$ from the escape
clause perturbing the answer prompt and $+0.4\pp$ from the re-retrieval
action itself.

Four further probes sharpen this picture.
\emph{First, the instruction's content cannot be pre-computed.} If a
one-sentence instruction that elicits sub-question reasoning is worth
$+5\pp$, can the memory service cache that reasoning---produce an evidence
plan (sub-questions, cited values, local conclusions) and hand it to a plain
reader? No: prepending such a plan costs $-5.6\pp$ under the default reader
and $-2.7\pp$ under the structured one (same-batch)---\emph{worse than no
scaffold at all}---and the more reasoning a scaffold carries, the more it
hurts (plan $-5.6$ vs state timeline $-1.6$; compression's $-3.0$ removes
context outright, a different failure). The reader inherits the plan's errors
and skips its own derivation: the instruction's value is eliciting the
reader's \emph{own} reasoning in the answering pass, not the reasoning
content. \emph{Second, the reasoning's presentation cannot be compiled.} We
compiled the same selected verbatim evidence into a reasoning-affordant
view---need-grouped quotes, occurrence lists, timelines, conflict and
missing-evidence panels, with conclusions and instructions expressly
forbidden---and handed it to the \emph{default} reader, targeting exactly the
questions the structured instruction fixes (default-wrong, structured-right;
$n{=}316$), with a same-batch raw-context control and a $299$-question
both-right stability sample. The view recovers nothing beyond the control's
spontaneous-recovery rate ($32.6\%$ vs $32.3\%$; a same-config re-run alone
re-fixes a third of the ``fixed'' set, so per-question flips are dominated by
borderline items) and \emph{breaks} $13.4\pp$ of previously-correct questions,
through mechanisms we verified against raw trajectories: appendix-scoped
absences asserted as data-wide facts, and fabricated citations---one audited
compilation promoted an environment's \emph{available-actions menu} to
executed events, with step numbers and quote marks---which the reader trusts
over the overview it still has. A hardened second iteration (subset-scoped
wording, computed deltas and absence claims forbidden, an explicit
option-vs-event rule, a deterministic verbatim-containment guard) still saw
the guard reject $69\%$ of compilations---instructions alone do not enforce
fidelity---and, deployed on the full $1{,}248$-question half, is exactly null
against its same-batch control ($0.5617$ vs $0.5641$ at a $31\%$ serve rate):
harmless, and worthless. \emph{Fidelity can be guarded; usefulness cannot be
compiled.} In hindsight the boundary could not have held: grouping asserts
relevance, a timeline asserts change, a missingness note asserts
nonexistence---every panel of a compiled view is an implicit conclusion, so a
view compiler is a query-time lossy \emph{constructor}, and the paper's core
claim applies to it recursively (the released paired case study shows wins and
losses flowing through the same mechanisms).

\emph{Third, the instruction's parts cannot be specialized by question type.}
Deterministic routing to purpose-built fragments of it (enumerate-then-count,
ledger-only state reconstruction, verbatim-copy, protective direct
answering---rules derived from an annotated 26-case study of what the
instruction fixes and breaks) scores $-3.0\pp$ overall: the routed classes
lose $-7$ to $-23\pp$ while the untouched-default remainder replicates its
anchor ($-0.3\pp$). The routing \emph{direction} was right---on the questions
the instruction breaks, routed fragments gain $+10.7\pp$---but the exchange
rate is ruinous: within the same classes, the questions the instruction
\emph{fixes} lose $-39\pp$, about ten collateral losses per targeted fix.
Every fragment ablates the full derive$\to$cite$\to$compose loop, which is
exactly how the instruction was quietly winning the body of each class; no
rule precision fixes a treatment whose side effect is removing the cure. (We
did not test additive per-class hints appended to the intact instruction.)
\emph{Fourth, a repair mechanism needs something specific to fetch.}
Re-retrieval cannot fix \emph{breadth} starvation: at a 6k budget (a known
$-9.5\pp$ gap), a separate sufficiency gate fires on $19\%$ of questions yet
recovers nothing ($-0.4\pp$)---the budget cut removes the overview, and
fetching more individual steps does not restore breadth.

\paragraph{The trust relay: repair versus override.}
The one composed mechanism that meets the single structured pass reuses the memory
service's own structured analysis: the reader is told to verify that analysis and
answer \emph{in full}, reaching $0.663$ on the full $2{,}496$-QA set ($+1.7$
$[+0.1,+3.3]$ over its same-batch structured anchor). It retains the structured
pass's wins and \emph{repairs} part of what that pass breaks; the residual losses
are independent re-adjudications, not compression ($78$--$95\%$ are overrides
despite the trust instruction), and the same re-judgment buys the repairs---a
full-set audit prices the exchange at $6.7\%$ of questions repaired against $4.5\%$
destroyed. Requiring the reader to \emph{name the contradicting step} before
overriding raises override precision to $0.672$ (vs $0.645$; $0.675$ on the half,
$+4.0$ $[+1.5,+6.5]$)---the strongest configuration we measure, indifferent to the
router base. It sits above the submitted single-pass system but is an internal
exploration, not part of the leaderboard entry.

\section{The deployed router: a post-hoc audit}
\label{app:routeraudit}

Preparing the release we found that the ``hybrid'' router in the deployed
configuration fuses lexical overlap with a second \emph{model-pick} signal (a short
LLM call that names likely-relevant turns)---\emph{not} the dense-embedding router
used on LOCOMO and LongMemEval-S. The harness leaves the model's thinking mode on (it never passes its \texttt{enable\_thinking} setting), so each $32$-token pick call
returns only the opening of an unfinished reasoning block (all $7{,}128$ such calls logged in two later re-runs), and the parser reads whatever numbers appear there as turns. We audited whether the signal contributes at all.

It does not. A same-day paired audit on the half set put a plain lexical base
\emph{ahead} ($+2.7\pp$ $[+0.5,+5.0]$); a pre-registered full-set replication is
score-neutral ($-0.8\pp$ $[-2.3,+0.8]$, the two bases trading domains; the lexical run scores $0.6466$).
A further full-set run of the model-pick hybrid base $+$ step-pin, made a day later on an
upgraded serving stack and therefore not paired with these, scores $0.6410$, $0.56\pp$ below
the lexical run and within the run-to-run spread of about $1\pp$. A pre-registered same-batch re-run on all $2{,}496$ QA then fixed the pick call (thinking off; prompt and $32$-token budget unchanged). The deployed configuration as published scores $0.5992$ there with the default answer prompt and $0.6486$ with the structured one; the fixed signal changes these by $+0.2\pp$ $[-1.5,+2.1]$ and $+1.4\pp$ $[-0.2,+3.2]$ (neither significant), and against lexical$+$pin it is $+1.8\pp$ $[+0.0,+3.6]$ and $+0.8\pp$ $[-0.8,+2.4]$, the two answer prompts disagreeing.
No reported \emph{difference} depends on the signal (memory configuration is
held constant within each same-batch table). We therefore name, recommend, and
release the reproducible \textbf{lexical $+$ step-pin} default: identical accuracy,
one fewer LLM call per retrieval, no malformed pick calls. This sharpens the
conclusion of \S\ref{sec:analysis}---on AMA-Bench the substrate and the structural
pin carry the routing, not a semantic scorer---and it is why we release
``lexical$+$pin'' as the default, while the tables report the deployed router the runs used.

budgets), pointing to budget-adaptive routing as an open lever. The converse
ablation removes query-time selection instead: answering from the recency

$0.6290$, same-day)---notably still above every constructed-memory system (the
\emph{extractive} overview already beats generative construction), but the routed
verbatim appendix clearly earns its keep.

\section{KV-serving correctness}
\label{app:kvcorrect}

This appendix substantiates the identity used in \S\ref{sec:method},
\S\ref{sec:analysis}, and \S\ref{sec:eff}: position-preserving KV gather
reproduces the full-prefill-then-mask oracle, so serving the selected spans from
the cache is the same computation as reading them inside the full trajectory---not
a re-prefill of them in isolation. Unless noted, the reader is a frozen dense
Qwen3-8B.

\paragraph{The mask-oracle gate.} The gathered KV is exactly the frozen model's
key/value for the selected turns at their original positions, so sub-selection
reproduces the full-prefill-then-mask oracle rather than a re-prefill of the
selected text alone (whose kept tokens would never have attended the dropped
context---a difference we measure as a $\sim\!13.5$-logit gap to a compact
re-prefill, $\sim\!20\times$ the residual below). Against the mask oracle the
first decoded token matches on every query: $100\%$ first-token argmax on
synthetic probes and on $144$ real AMA-Bench QA ($24/24$ episodes), at a
$\sim\!0.7$ residual logit gap that is the usual bf16 split-vs-joint-prefill
non-determinism.

\paragraph{Sequence-level, not just first-token.} Greedily decoding $256$ tokens,
sub-selection and the mask oracle emit the \emph{identical} full sequence on every
query at both a $3.2$k- and a $27$k-token trajectory, at first-position logit-KL
$\le\!0.011$ to the oracle (median $0$; versus $\sim\!0.28$ to a compact
re-prefill). The oracle's top-1-minus-top-2 answer margin averages $7.7$ (min
$0.25$), so these are not knife-edge decisions.

\paragraph{Robustness to kernel and RoPE extension.} The match holds under an
\emph{eager} attention kernel as well as SDPA ($6/6$), and under a $4\times$ YaRN
RoPE extension (first-position logit-KL $\le\!0.008$, argmax $5$--$6/6$)---as
expected, since position-preserving gather is exact for RoPE regardless of the
base frequency.

\paragraph{End-to-end parity, compact layout.} On real AMA under a consistent
reader/judge, KV sub-selection and the deployed text pipeline are tied within
noise ($-0.7\pp$, one QA of $144$). A stratified run confirms this at $n{=}576$ QA
over $72$ episodes across five domains (episodes $\le26$k tokens): the gate holds
at $24/24$, and the two paths remain accuracy-tied with KV marginally ahead under
both the same-model judge ($0.175$ vs $0.165$, $+1.0\pp$) and an independent
Qwen3-32B re-grade ($+0.7\pp$), at $81\%$ first-token agreement between the two
\emph{different} computations.

\paragraph{End-to-end parity, deployed layout.} The same holds when the KV arm
serves the \emph{deployed} layout: the recency$+$gist overview is prefilled once
per episode as a post-trajectory block (fixed positions, per-episode cacheable),
the selected spans are gathered from the trajectory cache, and only the question
is fresh. On a $576$-QA re-run ($\le24$k, four domains) the gate is again $24/24$
and the arms are tied ($0.222$ vs $0.240$, $-1.7\pp$, $\pm5\pp$ binomial band;
first-token agreement $90\%$). The two arms differ in block \emph{order}, so this
is parity between the deployed text pipeline and its cache-native equivalent, not
a byte-identical prompt; notably the overview lifts \emph{both} arms by $\sim\!5$
points, so the primitive covers the layout the headline numbers use.

\paragraph{Cross-backbone.} Re-running the harness on
\textbf{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}---a different architecture and vendor---reproduces
the guarantees: first-token argmax $6/6$ across $48$--$200$-step trajectories
(residual $|\Delta\text{logit}|\le0.35$, tighter than Qwen's $\sim\!0.7$) and
end-to-end match with the text re-prefill on all $6$ probes.


\section{Judge robustness}
\label{app:judge}

Our AMA-Bench reader is Qwen3-32B and the official harness scores it with a
Qwen3-32B judge, which raises the natural worry that a same-family judge inflates
the score. Two checks bound it.

First, the headline reproduces. A faithful re-run of the full official harness
scores $0.643$ under the Qwen3-32B judge ($95\%$ bootstrap CI $[0.623,0.662]$ over
the $2{,}496$ QA), within noise of the leaderboard-verified $0.6478$.

Second, the score survives a change of judge \emph{family}. Re-judging that run's
\emph{identical} predictions with an independent Llama-3.1-70B judge yields
$0.677$ $[0.658,0.695]$ at $84\%$ item-level agreement---higher than the Qwen
judge, so on our predictions the Qwen3-32B judge is the \emph{stricter} of the
two, not the more lenient. This is consistent with the official AMA-Bench
documentation, which reports a leniency bias for Qwen3-32B as a judge in
\emph{absolute} terms: because every leaderboard system is scored by that same
judge, our \emph{comparisons} sit on equal footing regardless of the absolute
level. We treat the cross-family re-judge as supportive rather than
definitive---it removes the same-family-inflation worry for our predictions
without our needing the absolute number to be judge-invariant.

\section{LOCOMO: protocol notes}
\label{app:benchcaveats}
We position LOCOMO as an \emph{out-of-domain} check rather than a headline claim, and
record the caveats here. Under the public gpt-4o-mini judge, full-history prompting
scores $0.756$---above \kvm{}'s $0.704$---while under a consistent Qwen3-32B judge the
comparison flips ($0.716 > 0.661$); the two judges disagree on exactly this
full-vs-selection comparison, which is why we report both and treat the
consistent-judge result as the cleaner one (a full-history prompt is not an oracle: it
carries the distractors that hurt the reader). The benchmark also has known protocol
variants (QA subsets, judge choices), and newer file- and tool-based systems report
stronger numbers under their own setups (e.g.\ filesystem-backed agents at
$0.740$~\citep{lettafiles} and $0.744$~\citep{pamlocomo}). The signal we draw is
narrower and survives these caveats: under one fixed protocol, compact verbatim
selection is competitive with dedicated dialogue-memory systems and, under a
consistent judge, beats prompting the full history.
